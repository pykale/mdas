# What is MDAS?

The Multimodal Data Analysis Session (MDAS): Cancer & Cardiac Research introduces practical workflows for working with different types of biomedical data. The Cancer and Cardiac libraries are built on [PyKale](https://github.com/pykale/pykale).

## Purpose and research focus

Explore how multimodal machine learning can combine biomedical data types, and gain confidence adapting example workflows to research questions in cancer and cardiac applications.

- **Cancer:** Combine clinical information and pathology patch features to explore time-to-event prediction with a synthetic cohort.
- **Cardiac:** Explore ECG leads and chest X-ray/ECG fusion through synthetic-data demonstration workflows.
- **Your own research:** Discuss which data modalities, outcomes and practical constraints matter in your project.

## Who should attend?

AI/ML researchers, data scientists, research software engineers, clinicians, clinical researchers and PhD students working with, or planning to work with, cancer or cardiac data.

Some Python experience and a laptop are recommended. Clinicians can focus on what data are used, what is predicted, how results are interpreted and where limitations arise. Technical participants can also inspect configurations and code.

## Multimodal fusion and the analysis workflow

The session introduces **early, intermediate and late fusion** at a high level before participants explore practical examples. Follow the workflow from data preparation and loading through encoding, fusion, prediction, evaluation and interpretation.

The Cancer track uses intermediate fusion in a Cox time-to-event model. The Cardiac track explores multimodal representation learning and lead-specific ECG workflows. Both start with synthetic data so participants can explore without a patient dataset.

## What you will learn

By the end of the session, participants should be better able to:

1. Identify relevant modalities and a research endpoint.
2. Follow a documented example end to end.
3. Distinguish a synthetic pipeline check from clinically meaningful evaluation.
4. Explain what a fusion strategy changes and what a reported metric means.
5. Identify the next steps and constraints for applying a workflow to their own data.
