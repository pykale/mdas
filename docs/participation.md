# Participation Guide

Join us on **Wednesday, 18 November 2026, 13:00–17:00**.

**Venue:** Ada Lovelace Room (108), Level 1, Regent Court, University of Sheffield.

## Who this is for

Researchers, clinicians, data scientists, research software engineers and PhD students who want to understand a multimodal analysis workflow. Basic Python helps: you should be comfortable with imports, functions and reading a table. Clinicians can follow the research question, predictions and interpretation without writing every line of code.

## What to bring and how to prepare

- Bring a laptop with a modern browser.
- Bring a research question. Think about the outcome you want to study and what measurements are available for each participant.
- If you plan to use your own data, email [mdas-team-group@sheffield.ac.uk](mailto:mdas-team-group@sheffield.ac.uk) with the data modalities you plan to bring. Install the Cancer or Cardiac repo locally before the session using [Quick Start](quick-start.md).
- If you’re using the supplied synthetic examples, you don’t need to bring a dataset.

## In person or at your own pace

At the session, use Part 1 to learn the pipeline and Part 2 to apply it to your own research question or to data provided by the organisers, with support from the AIRE team. The [programme](about.md#programme) contains the detailed programme.

Learning independently? Choose one track, complete a synthetic run, and record the endpoint, modalities, outputs and one limitation. Read the interpretation guidance on the example page before changing a configuration. The same materials remain available after the event.

## Synthetic data and privacy

Synthetic results show that a pipeline runs; they do not establish clinical usefulness. Do not upload identifiable patient data, confidential research, credentials or private datasets to public services or this repository. Before using your own data, discuss permissions, secure infrastructure, expected data layout and compute needs with the organisers or your research team.
